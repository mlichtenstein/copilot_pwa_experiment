Sunset: solid clouds = cloudy air that is still rising warm, or pressed flat under warmer air above it (the inversion), lit peach and pink from a low sun at lower right and grey-violet in their own shadow, with frayed wisps at the edges; faint veil = still cloud and near-saturated haze; soft curtains = falling rain.

The rules and update formulas

Each pixel holds temperature (0 cold to 1 hot), vapor, cloud, rain and spin (vorticity). Each floor column also has a puddle. These are experimental units, not degrees or grams. With altitude from 0 at the floor to 1 at the lid, each tick:

1. Floor and lid. floorT += heating × (1 + flicker × (random − 0.5) − floorT); lidT += cooling × (0 − lidT). Puddles evaporate: lift = min(puddle, 0.2 × (0.4 − floorVapor)), which moves water into floor vapor and cools it by latentHeat × lift.

1b. Inversion (Options, on by default). At and above inversionHeight (0.6), temperature += 0.02 × (target − temperature) with target = 0.4 + 0.35 × smoothstep((altitude − inversionHeight) / 0.1): cool at the base, warm and stable from 0.1 higher up to the lid. Rising air from the mixed layer below meets warmer air, loses its buoyancy and spreads sideways; the warm layer is also dry, since warm air holds more vapor.

2. Clouds. capacity = exp(3 × (temperature − 1 − expansion × altitude)). Above capacity, 0.25 × excess condenses into liquid and warms the pixel by latentHeat per unit. Below capacity, cloud evaporates at evaporation × deficit and rain at 0.03 × deficit, cooling it. Cloud above 0.005 turns into rain: drops = autoconversion × (cloud − 0.005).

3. Buoyancy. lightness = temperature − 0.4 × (cloud + rain); spin −= buoyancy × d(lightness)/dx, then 0.4% drag and viscosity sharing with neighbors. A warm pixel beside a cool one starts the air turning, up on the warm side.

4. Flow. A stream function solves ∇²ψ = −spin (24 over-relaxed sweeps, walls closed). Flow across each pixel edge is the difference of ψ at its two corners, so air is never created or destroyed. The fastest edge is capped at 0.24 pixels per tick.

5. Transport. Temperature, vapor, cloud, rain and spin move with the flow by upwind edge fluxes; what leaves one pixel enters its neighbor. Temperature also diffuses at 0.01.

6. Rain. falling = fallSpeed × rain drops one pixel, outrunning the 0.24 px/tick updrafts. Rain leaving the bottom row fills that column’s puddle.

Sunset view. With cloudStyle Cumulus, the simulation's cloud water is drawn as cloud only where the air is also buoyant (a rising puff, as cumulus are) or capped (pressed under warmer air, as stratocumulus are): puff = min(1, cloud / 0.004) × max(min(1, max(0, buoyancy) / 0.06), min(1, max(0, T₄ above − T) / 0.1)), where T₄ above is the temperature 4 pixels higher. The puff field is blurred over about 3 pixels, so thin streamers fade and masses round off, scaled by a fixed smooth lobe pattern (±45%, lobes about 6 pixels across, the same on every seed), and passed through a soft threshold from 0.25 to 0.42 to give an opaque body with a defined, billowed edge. With feather on, a wider blur (about 4.5 pixels) of the same puff field, scaled by 1 + 1.4 × fray (fractal noise, octaves 8, 4 and 2 pixels) and soft-thresholded from 0.04 to 0.4, adds a fringe up to 75% opaque that frays out over several pixels around the solid core. Shading treats the blurred cover as a rounded surface lit by a low sun off the lower right, and dims it by exp(−0.06 × cover crossed along 40 pixels toward the sun), mixing from grey-violet (shaded) to peach near the floor or pink near the lid (lit). Behind the puffs: a fixed dusk gradient, indigo at the lid to gold at the floor, brightened or dimmed up to 10% by buoyancy; a lit haze up to 12% from 90% relative humidity; and still cloud water as a veil up to 12%. Rain is drawn over it all in the cloud's light at up to 50% opacity, full at 0.008. With cloudStyle Raw cloud water, every bit of cloud is drawn by its own amount instead, opacity = 1 − exp(−(cloud / 0.004 + 0.15 × haze²)), shadowed toward the sun; it reads as smoke. This is drawing only: the simulated water, cloud and rain are the same in every view and style.

The loop: heated floor air rises, expands and condenses. Condensing releases heat, so the cloud keeps rising. The lid cools it and rain falls out. Rain evaporating below cools the air, which sinks. Puddles re-wet the floor. Total water stays constant, and the random floor flicker keeps the blobs from settling into fixed rolls. Changing any slider or option except water affects the current run.
