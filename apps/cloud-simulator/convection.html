The rules and update formulas

Each pixel holds temperature (0 cold to 1 hot), vapor, cloud, rain and spin (vorticity). Each floor column also has a puddle. These are experimental units, not degrees or grams. With altitude from 0 at the floor to 1 at the lid, each tick:

1. Floor and lid. floorT += heating × (1 + flicker × (random − 0.5) − floorT); lidT += cooling × (0 − lidT). Puddles evaporate: lift = min(puddle, 0.2 × max(0, 0.4 − floorVapor)), which moves water into floor vapor and cools it by latentHeat × lift. With the LCL, the target is the surface dewpoint instead, capacity at lclHeight (using that row's mean temperature), and the exchange runs both ways: air moister than that returns water to the puddle as dew.

Grid size (Options: resolution). Pixel art 64×48 (default) or Detailed 128×96; switching restarts the same seed and parameters and sets pixelArt to match. Pixel-counted lengths and speeds scale by gridScale = width / 128: the 0.24 px/tick speed limit, rain fall min(1, fallSpeed × gridScale), bubble radii, and the Sunset view's blurs, reaches, noise sizes and sun path, so the numbers below are for 128×96.

Layering (Options): None, Inversion (1b) or LCL (1c, the default).

1b. Inversion (Layering: Inversion). At and above inversionHeight (0.6), temperature += 0.02 × (target − temperature) with target = 0.4 + 0.35 × smoothstep((altitude − inversionHeight) / 0.1): cool at the base, warm and stable from 0.1 higher up to the lid. Rising air from the mixed layer below meets warmer air, loses its buoyancy and spreads sideways; the warm layer is also dry, since warm air holds more vapor.

1c. LCL (Layering: LCL). Capacity is judged at each row's mean temperature with a steeper lapse, capacity = 2 × exp(3 × (rowMeanT − 1 − 1 × altitude)) (the 2 is a moist air mass, as in the tropical trades), since the toy's plumes are far warmer than real thermals (a fraction of a degree) and would otherwise saturate higher than the air around them. Scanning up from the floor while the layer's mean vapor is still below the next row's capacity finds the mixed subcloud layer; its vapor relaxes toward the layer mean, vapor += 0.05 × (layerMean − vapor), which moves water but never makes it. Every parcel leaving that layer carries the same vapor, so it saturates at the same height: a flat cloud base, with clear air under it. Above the base, condensation warms the rising air and keeps the tops buoyant. Cloud evaporates faster here, 0.3 × deficit, so cloud sinking below the base or mixed out at a top dries quickly. With drier air (no factor of 2) the clouds hold too little water and their rain all evaporates in the clear layer under the bases (virga).

2. Clouds. capacity = exp(3 × (temperature − 1 − expansion × altitude)). Above capacity, 0.25 × excess condenses into liquid and warms the pixel by latentHeat per unit. Below capacity, cloud evaporates at evaporation × deficit and rain at 0.03 × deficit, cooling it. Cloud above 0.005 turns into rain: drops = autoconversion × (cloud − 0.005).

3. Buoyancy. lightness = temperature − 0.4 × (cloud + rain); spin −= buoyancy × d(lightness)/dx, then 0.4% drag and viscosity sharing with neighbors. A warm pixel beside a cool one starts the air turning, up on the warm side.

4. Flow. A stream function solves ∇²ψ = −spin (24 over-relaxed sweeps, walls closed). Flow across each pixel edge is the difference of ψ at its two corners, so air is never created or destroyed. The fastest edge is capped at 0.24 pixels per tick.

5. Transport. Temperature, vapor, cloud, rain and spin move with the flow by upwind edge fluxes; what leaves one pixel enters its neighbor. Temperature also diffuses at 0.01.

6. Rain. falling = fallSpeed × rain drops one pixel, outrunning the 0.24 px/tick updrafts. Rain leaving the bottom row fills that column’s puddle.

Sunset view. With cloudStyle Cumulus, the simulation's cloud water is drawn as cloud only where the air is also buoyant or rising (a puff, as cumulus are) or capped (pressed under warmer air, as stratocumulus are): puff = min(1, cloud / 0.004) × max(min(1, max(0, buoyancy) / 0.06), min(1, max(0, T₄ above − T) / 0.1), min(1, max(0, updraft) / 0.05)), where T₄ above is the temperature 4 pixels higher and updraft is the upward flow in pixels per tick. The puff field is blurred over about 3 pixels, so thin streamers fade and masses round off, scaled by a fixed smooth lobe pattern (±45%, lobes about 6 pixels across, the same on every seed), and passed through a soft threshold from 0.25 to 0.42 to give an opaque body with a defined, billowed edge. With feather on, a wider blur (about 4.5 pixels) of the same puff field, scaled by 1 + 1.4 × fray (fractal noise, octaves 8, 4 and 2 pixels) and soft-thresholded from 0.04 to 0.4, adds a fringe up to 75% opaque that frays out over several pixels around the solid core. Under a cloud (core 3 pixels above, none 3 below) the fringe is cut back by that difference, so bases stay crisp while tops and sides fray. Shading treats the blurred cover as a rounded surface lit by a low sun off the lower right, and dims it by exp(−0.06 × cover crossed along 40 pixels toward the sun), mixing from grey-violet (shaded) to peach near the floor or pink near the lid (lit). Behind the puffs: a fixed dusk gradient, indigo at the lid to gold at the floor, brightened or dimmed up to 10% by buoyancy; a lit haze up to 12% from 90% relative humidity; and still cloud water as a veil up to 12%. Rain is drawn over it all in the cloud's light at up to 50% opacity, full at 0.008. With cloudStyle Raw cloud water, every bit of cloud is drawn by its own amount instead, opacity = 1 − exp(−(cloud / 0.004 + 0.15 × haze²)), shadowed toward the sun; it reads as smoke. With pixelArt on: the sky is 3 flat bands per color step, ordered-dithered (4×4 Bayer, dither in 0 to 1) over 35% of each band at its edges; cloud light steps to 4 levels and tint to 3 (no warmth tint); the core is solid above 0.5; the fringe is solid where cover > 0.2 + 0.6 × dither; rain is a 50% curtain where rain opacity > 0.1 + 0.4 × dither. This is drawing only: the simulated water, cloud and rain are the same in every view and style.

The loop: heated floor air rises, expands and condenses. Condensing releases heat, so the cloud keeps rising. The lid cools it and rain falls out. Rain evaporating below cools the air, which sinks. Puddles re-wet the floor. Total water stays constant, and the random floor flicker keeps the blobs from settling into fixed rolls. Changing any slider or option except water affects the current run.
