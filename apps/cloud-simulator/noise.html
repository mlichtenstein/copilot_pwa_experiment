The rules and formulas

Nothing here is simulated physics except the rain. humidity, temperature and pressure are each a 2D slice of its own seeded 3D Perlin noise, with time as the third axis, sliding sideways with the wind: value = bias + amplitude × 2 × fractal((x − scroll) / size, y / size, time). size is featureSize (×1.5 for temperature, ×2 for pressure); time grows by changeSpeed per tick (temperature and pressure change more slowly); scroll grows by wind. Humidity's bias is cloudiness; the other noise settings are fixed.

capacity = exp(3 × (temperature − 1)) × (1 + 0.3 × pressure), so cold air and low pressure hold less; relative = humidity / capacity.

Cloud layer. With altitude 0 at the floor and 1 at the lid, band = exp(−(|altitude − layerHeight| / w)^layerSharpness), where the half-width w is layerThickness / 2 × (1 + layerAsymmetry) above the middle and × (1 − layerAsymmetry) below. It is 1 in the middle and falls to 0 above and below. cloud = clamp((relative × band − 1) × 8, 0, 1), so clouds form only inside the layer, with a crisper bottom when the asymmetry is positive.

Rain. Cloud that stays together builds charge (+0.02 × cloud per tick, up to 1; clear air drains it 4% per tick). Past 0.6 it makes rainAmount × cloud rain per tick. Every tick all rain moves fallSpeed pixels down and wind pixels sideways, split over neighboring pixels, and outside cloud 1.5% of it dries each tick. Rain leaving the floor fills that column’s puddle, which dries at 0.2% per tick. Rain made = rain in the air + puddles + evaporated, exactly.

Sunset view. Nine dithered sky bands and cloud in four shades lit from a low sun off the lower right. Rain is drawn separately as thin streaks, 8–18 pixels long, born where the rain field is: each slides down at fallSpeed, leans and drifts with the wind, and fades in and out over its life and toward both ends, drawn as sky-tinted grey.
