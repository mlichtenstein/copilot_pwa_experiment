Cloud simulator / Experiment 04

Noise weather

Humidity, heat and pressure drift as noise through time. Everything drifts with one wind. Clouds condense fast where the air saturates inside a cloud layer and linger after; dense, lasting clouds rain, and the rain falls all the way to the floor.

The rules and formulas

The noise is only the forcing; cloud water and rain are stored and evolve. humidity, temperature and pressure are each a 2D slice of its own seeded 3D Perlin noise, with time as the third axis, sliding sideways with the wind: value = bias + amplitude × 2 × fractal((x − scroll) / size, y / size, time). size is featureSize (×1.5 for temperature, ×2 for pressure); time grows by changeSpeed per tick (temperature and pressure change more slowly); scroll grows by wind. Humidity's bias is cloudiness; the other noise settings are fixed.

capacity = exp(3 × (temperature − 1)) × (1 + 0.3 × pressure), so cold air and low pressure hold less; relative = humidity / capacity.

Cloud layer. With altitude 0 at the floor and 1 at the lid, band = exp(−(|altitude − layerHeight| / w)^1.5), where the half-width w is layerThickness / 2 × (1 + layerAsymmetry) above the middle and × (1 − layerAsymmetry) below. It is 1 in the middle and fades softly to 0 above and below (the fixed exponent 1.5 gives no flat top or hard edge). potential = clamp((relative × band − 1) × 8, 0, 1), so clouds form only inside the layer, with a crisper bottom when the asymmetry is positive.

Cloud water. Each pixel stores cloud water, carried by the wind. Where the potential is higher it condenses fast (30% of the gap per tick); where it is lower it evaporates slowly and from the edges in: loss = 0.04 × 10^(−1.5 × persistence) × (0.05 + 10 × exposure) per tick, where exposure is the mean cloud missing from the 8 neighboring pixels (0 deep inside a cloud, about 0.4 on a straight edge, 1 for a lone pixel). So a fading cloud keeps a dense core and shrinks as its edge eats inward.

Rain. Cloud water of at least 0.8 builds charge (+0.012 per tick, up to 1; anywhere thinner it drains 10% per tick). Past 0.6, about 50 ticks of staying dense, it makes up to rainAmount rain per tick (more the denser it is) and loses twice that in cloud water, so a cloud the noise no longer feeds rains itself thin, stops raining and then fades. Every tick all rain moves fallSpeed pixels down and wind pixels sideways, split over neighboring pixels, carried in whole pixels with the cloud it fell from, and each pixel passes 0.3% of itself to each side neighbor, so a shaft widens only slightly. Rain never dries in the air: it falls until it leaves the floor into that column’s puddle, which dries at 0.2% per tick. Rain made = rain in the air + puddles + evaporated, exactly.

Sunset view. Nine dithered sky bands and cloud in four shades lit from a low sun off the lower right. Rain is drawn from the rain in the air two ways, neither of which changes the simulation, so every shaft can be followed from the floor up to its cloud. A dithered grey curtain covers wherever rain is falling, denser where there is more: it starts inside the raining cloud, evening out the cloud's lower part, and runs to the floor; when a cloud stops raining the top of its curtain lets go and falls away. Thin streaks of random length (3–30 pixels, mostly short) are born anywhere in the falling rain, more where it is heavy: each slides down at about fallSpeed, leans and drifts with the wind, and fades in and out over its life, drawn as sky-tinted grey.

Drift. Each slider follows its own Gaussian random walk with no pull back toward where it started or was last set, so it can roam its whole range: at normal speed typically about a fifth of the range in 16 seconds of play, half in under two minutes and all of it in about seven. It bounces off the ends of its range, its only limits. It uses its own random numbers, so the weather seed is unchanged, and it only moves while playing.
