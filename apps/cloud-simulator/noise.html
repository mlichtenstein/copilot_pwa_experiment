The rules and formulas

Nothing here is simulated physics except the rain. Each of humidity, temperature, pressure and wind is a 2D slice of its own seeded 3D Perlin noise, with time as the third axis: value = bias + amplitude × 2 × fractal(x / scale, y / scale, tick × speed), where fractal adds octaves layers, each twice as fine and roughness times as strong. The fields move smoothly forever and never settle.

With altitude 0 at the floor and 1 at the lid: temperature −= lapse × (altitude − 0.5) and humidity −= drying × (altitude − 0.5). capacity = exp(3 × (temperature − 1)) × (1 + pressureLift × pressure), so cold air and low pressure hold less. relative humidity = humidity / capacity; cloud = clamp((relative − cloudThreshold) × cloudGain, 0, 1).

Rain. Cloud that stays in one place builds charge += 0.02 × cloud per tick (up to 1); clear air lets it drain by 4% per tick. Past rainThreshold, the pixel makes rainRate × cloud rain per tick. Rain outside cloud dries by rainEvaporation × (1 − relative) of itself per tick. Then fallSpeed of each pixel’s rain moves down one pixel, and windDrift × wind of that moving rain slides one pixel sideways (wrapping at the edges). Rain leaving the floor fills that column’s puddle, which dries at 0.2% per tick. Rain made = rain in the air + puddles + evaporated, exactly.

Sunset view. Nine dithered sky bands, indigo at the lid to gold at the floor. Cloud is drawn in four shades: lit peach where it thins toward a low sun off the lower right, grey-violet where it thickens. Its soft edge dithers out. Rain is drawn as dashes sliding down one pixel per tick.
