The lighting rules and formulas

Each pixel’s density is extruded through a slab of thickness slabDepth. The camera looks straight through its front. The sun is distant, behind the slab; sunAngle rotates its direction in the image plane with a fixed depth component of 0.6. This is a 2.5D experiment.

tau = extinction × integral(density × pathLength)
T = exp(−tau)

For each camera-ray sample, trace a second ray toward the sun to compute Tsun. Accumulate single-scattered light:

Lcloud += Tcamera × Tsun × density × extinction × albedo × phase × Lsun × stepLength

phase = (1 − g²) / (4π × (1 + g² − 2g × cosTheta)^(3/2)). Here cosTheta = 0.6; directions point along incoming and outgoing light propagation. The renderer uses 4π × phase with relative sunlight units.

airMass = 1 / max(0.04, sin(sunAngle))
LsunRGB = visibility × exp(−airOpticalDepth × [0.35, 0.75, 1.6] × airMass)

RGB extinction approximates stronger short-wavelength scattering. Sunlight fades below the horizon; air mass is clamped near the horizon. These are artistic approximations, not a calibrated atmosphere.

Lcamera = Tcamera × Lbackground + Lcloud + ambient × (1 − Tcamera)
display = (1 − exp(−exposure × Lcamera))^(1/2.2)

Cloud paths use six camera and eight sunlight samples. The sky gradient, sun disk and ambient term are approximations. No multiple scattering, refraction, evolving droplets or full spectral transport. Density never changes during playback. Playback moves 5 degrees/second from 170 to −8 and loops.

Inspired by PBRT transmittance and phase functions.
