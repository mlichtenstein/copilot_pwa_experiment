The lighting rules and formulas

Each pixel’s density is extruded through a slab of thickness slabDepth. The camera looks straight through its front. The sun is distant, behind the slab. It follows the shared figure-eight sun path (sunPhase): its azimuth, spread over ±45° across the picture, and its elevation set its direction in the image plane, with a fixed depth component of 0.6. This is a 2.5D experiment.

tau = extinction × integral(density × pathLength)
T = exp(−tau)

For each camera-ray sample, trace a second ray toward the sun to compute Tsun. Accumulate single-scattered light:

Lcloud += Tcamera × Tsun × density × extinction × albedo × phase × Lsun × stepLength

phase = (1 − g²) / (4π × (1 + g² − 2g × cosTheta)^(3/2)). Here cosTheta = 0.6; directions point along incoming and outgoing light propagation. The renderer uses 4π × phase with relative sunlight units.

airMass = 1 / max(0.04, sin(elevation))
LsunRGB = visibility × exp(−airOpticalDepth × [0.35, 0.75, 1.6] × airMass)

RGB extinction approximates stronger short-wavelength scattering. Sunlight fades below the horizon; air mass is clamped near the horizon. These are artistic approximations, not a calibrated atmosphere.

Lcamera = Tcamera × Lbackground + Lcloud + ambient × (1 − Tcamera)
display = (1 − exp(−exposure × Lcamera))^(1/2.2)

Cloud paths use six camera and eight sunlight samples. The sky gradient, sun disk and ambient term are approximations. No multiple scattering, refraction, evolving droplets or full spectral transport. Density never changes during playback. Play sun runs the shared clock: one loop of the figure-eight takes 90 seconds, swinging the sun from 40° left to 40° right and dipping it from 25° up to 5° below the horizon twice per loop, in every experiment that has a sun.

Inspired by PBRT transmittance and phase functions.
