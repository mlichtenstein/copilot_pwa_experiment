Cloud patterns

How the pictures combine

Three fixed cloud pictures sit at depths 22, 50 and 78, with 32×20, 64×40 and 96×60 cells. Farther pictures cover more world space. The camera uses fixed perspective (58°); each density cell spans 4 world units.

Between each pair is one 64×40 air picture. For each of its pixels, project toward the sun onto the farther cloud pictures. Their densities determine how much sunlight gets through:

sunTransmission = product(exp(−cloudOpacity × density / sunDirection.z))

Use that light to color the air picture, then blend everything from far to near:

airTransmission = exp(−haze × gapDepth × 0.04)
airColor = (1 − airTransmission) × sunTransmission × sunColor
color = background × airTransmission + airColor

Cloud pictures similarly use cloudTransmission = exp(−cloudOpacity × density) to blend their lit color over what is behind them. Low sunlight uses the previous experiment’s approximate RGB atmospheric attenuation.

This is a layered image approximation: one lighting sample per air picture, no 3D air grid, slice, phase controls or volume ray marching. Cloud shading, sky and display tone mapping are approximate. Only the sun moves during playback; pattern buttons change one cloud picture at a time. Reset restores the original patterns and lighting.
