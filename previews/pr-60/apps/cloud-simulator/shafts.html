Geometry, lighting rules and approximations

The box spans x = −48…48, y = −30…30, z = 0…96. A 64 × 40 × 24 grid holds three cloud sheets centered at z = 22, 50, 78, each 4 units thick. The perspective camera is at (0, 0, −12), looking along +z; nearby sheets appear larger. Rays leave the finite box into vacuum.

sigma = haze + cloudExtinction × density × layerMultiplier

Sweep from far z toward the camera. Each cell reads the transmitted light from its upstream neighbors in the previous depth slice (bilinear interpolation):

pathLength = 4 / sunDirection.z
Tcenter = Tupstream × exp(−sigma × pathLength / 2)
Tout = Tupstream × exp(−sigma × pathLength)

Open sun-facing boundaries receive unit light. This shared sweep replaces per-camera-sample shadow rays. Oblique shadows soften through interpolation; the grid is intentionally coarse. Sun directions are restricted to the forward hemisphere so the sweep stays well behaved.

Camera rays sample the cached sunlight and extinction once per depth slice:

Tsegment = exp(−sigma × cameraStepLength)
L += Tcamera × (1 − Tsegment) × scattering / sigma × Tcenter × Lsun
Tcamera *= Tsegment

scattering combines haze with Henyey–Greenstein parameter g and cloud scattering with fixed g = 0.45, albedo = 0.95. Haze uses RGB weights [0.65, 0.9, 1.3]; the same scalar extinction is used in all channels. Air-only view omits cloud scattering and backdrop, but keeps cloud shadows and camera extinction.

Relative solar RGB follows the prior experiment’s approximate atmospheric attenuation. Background, sun disk, RGB haze weights and display tone mapping are artistic approximations. No multiple scattering, ground shadows, physical units or evolving clouds. Coarse sheet interpolation and finite side boundaries can introduce artifacts.

Playback starts paused and moves only sunAzimuth at 3 degrees/second, bouncing at ±40°. Controls and diagnostic views do not evolve density.
