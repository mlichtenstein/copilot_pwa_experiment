The rules and update formulas

Each pixel holds temperature (0 cold to 1 hot), vapor, liquid (cloud and rain) and spin (vorticity). Each floor column also has a puddle. These are experimental units, not degrees or grams. With altitude from 0 at the floor to 1 at the lid, each tick:

1. Floor and lid. floorT += heating × (1 + flicker × (random − 0.5) − floorT); lidT += cooling × (0 − lidT). Puddles evaporate: lift = min(puddle, 0.2 × (0.4 − floorVapor)), which moves water into floor vapor and cools it by latentHeat × lift.

2. Clouds. capacity = exp(3 × (temperature − 1 − expansion × altitude)). Above capacity, 0.25 × excess condenses into liquid and warms the pixel by latentHeat per unit. Below capacity, liquid evaporates at evaporation × deficit and cools it.

3. Buoyancy. lightness = temperature − 0.4 × liquid; spin −= buoyancy × d(lightness)/dx, then 0.4% drag and viscosity sharing with neighbors. A warm pixel beside a cool one starts the air turning, up on the warm side.

4. Flow. A stream function solves ∇²ψ = −spin (24 over-relaxed sweeps, walls closed). Flow across each pixel edge is the difference of ψ at its two corners, so air is never created or destroyed. The fastest edge is capped at 0.24 pixels per tick.

5. Transport. Temperature, vapor, liquid and spin move with the flow by upwind edge fluxes; what leaves one pixel enters its neighbor. Temperature also diffuses at 0.01.

6. Rain. falling = fallSpeed × max(0, liquid − 0.01) drops one pixel. Rain leaving the bottom row fills that column’s puddle.

The loop: heated floor air rises, expands and condenses. Condensing releases heat, so the cloud keeps rising. The lid cools it and rain falls out. Rain evaporating below cools the air, which sinks. Puddles re-wet the floor. Total water stays constant, and the seeded floor flicker keeps the blobs from settling into fixed rolls. Changing any slider except water affects the current run.
