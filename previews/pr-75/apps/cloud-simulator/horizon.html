Geometry and lighting rules

Near to far, the six scene layers are terrain at 3 km, cloud at 8 km, terrain at 18 km, cloud at 30 km, terrain at 65 km, cloud at 90 km, multiplied by distanceScale. Three literal grayscale bitmaps repeat across the cloud planes; density and shapes never evolve.

The camera is 150 m above an Earth-sized sphere (radius 6371 km), pitched up 5° with 40° vertical field of view. cloudAltitude sets the central cloud height. altitudeSpread shifts near/middle/far centers by −0.7/0/+0.7 times the spread, adds fixed per-instance height offsets, and sets vertical bitmap thickness to 0.5 + 0.45 × spread km. The actual altitude is clamped above sea level.

For every visible part of a cloud, trace toward the distant sun. If that line intersects Earth, direct light is zero. Otherwise, integrate through an atmosphere extending to 100 km:

molecularDensity(h) = exp(−h / 8 km)
aerosolDensity(h) = exp(−h / 1.2 km)
sunRGB = exp(−integral(extinctionRGB × pathLength))

Sea-level RGB molecular coefficients are [0.0058, 0.0135, 0.0331] per km, plus a neutral aerosol term. Blue is scattered out of the direct beam more strongly; long sunlight paths become redder. Higher clouds can stay sunlit after lower clouds enter Earth's shadow. Curvature also changes the local horizon with distance.

The sky accumulates coarse single scattering along view rays, using Rayleigh and approximate aerosol phase functions. The camera-to-layer path adds wavelength-dependent extinction and a sky-colored aerial-perspective approximation. Each cloud pixel is colored independently, so top and bottom can differ.

Sun distance is effectively infinite; its angular position moves below the horizon. The sun follows the shared vertical figure-eight path used by every experiment with a sun: sunPhase places it on the path and the four sunPath* sliders size and place the loop. Play sunset runs the shared clock around it. Only light moves. Numerical sampling, RGB rather than full spectra, fixed cloud shading, terrain profiles, aerial perspective and a small night/cloud ambient fill are approximations. No refraction, ozone, multiple scattering, weather or terrain-cast sun shadows; terrain only occludes the camera view. Full twilight colors may therefore differ from reality.

References: GPU Gems atmospheric scattering and Bruneton atmospheric scattering. This is a small independent CPU approximation, not those implementations.
